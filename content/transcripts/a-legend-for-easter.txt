This legend of the first Easter eggs is one of drama and adventure and faith in God. Long ago in feudal Europe, Rosalind, the beautiful daughter of the Duke of Burgundy, had two handsome suitors, Hano and Arno. Marry me and I will give you wealth, a castle and a title. I have neither wealth nor position to offer, but I love you, Rosalind. Rosalind loved Arno too. They were married much to the envy of Hano. They shall have no happiness together. I will see to that. Five years of happiness passed for Rosalind and Arno. They had two children, a boy named Edmund and a girl named Blondah. All was serene in their happy home until one day at dinner a guard came in. Lord Arno, the Muslims are invading our southern borders. Unless you lead the village forces against them, our land will be overrun. Hano is captain of the guard. Why appeal to me? Hano had plotted this scheme. He tarried while Arno went to war to save the country. Do he calls goodbye, Rosalind? God speed you and bless you, Arno. Not realizing that this was a trap to separate him from his family, Arno led his countrymen to the southern border. He was unaware that Hano was furnishing the Muslims with money and supplies. Meanwhile, Rosalind and the children were in grave danger. Lady Rosalind, there are five strangers in the village. They asked where you lived. Zelda, I fear our safety. Describe the men to me. Rosalind listened carefully. She had heard of Hano's intrigue then. Come, children. We must flee or we shall be made captive. Then they left their home. They walked on and on through the forest. Marry our mother. Protect us. At dusk, they reached a clearing. Rosalind went to a miller's cottage by a cliff and knocked. Sir, may we please have refuge for the night? The miller and his wife gladly gave the three wanderers lodging for the night. If you have no shelter, you may share our home as long as you wish. God bless you for your kindness, sir. How can I ever thank you? A year passed without word of Arno. Rosalind was happy in the miller's home. Edmond and Blonda, too, had found good friends. Rosalind planned an Easter party for her children and their playmates. What will you give them to eat, the Easter party? No fruits or berries are ripe during this season. We can give them eggs, but they're colorless. Rosalind worked to make the party gay, painting eggs with bright flowers and verses. The party was great fun. All the children saved the pretty eggs. One boy took his along when he visited an aunt, twenty miles away. There he showed it to a passing pilgrim who read the verse aloud, To thee our earthly food we owe grant us, O Lord, thy gifts to know. Where did you get this egg, son? From a nice lady. She left over there. Together they sped toward Rosalind's house where the pilgrim flung aside his cloak, and clad in armor faced his wife. Rosalind, I thought I should never find you. It is you, Arno. Thank God you are safe. I will always keep this precious egg as a symbol of your kind heart. God rewarded you for making Easter happy and holy for the little children about you. Let us have a party every Easter, Arno, and color the eggs. Wonderful. Mother, can you color the Easter eggs? So, the beautiful custom, originating with Rosalind and Arno, soon spread from village to village, and today is known around the world.
