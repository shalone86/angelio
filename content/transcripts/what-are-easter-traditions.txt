During Easter, we celebrate the resurrection of Jesus Christ. The celebration involves various customs and traditions such as the Pascal, candle, baptisms, Easter eggs, and the Easter fire. Welcome to SD Case & Courses. Today's topic is Easter traditions. Liturgical practices during Easter include the Easter Vigil, the Baptism and Confirmation of Adults, the Renewal of Baptismal Promises, the Paschal Triduum, which includes Holy Thursday, Good Friday and Holy Saturday, and the Easter season which starts on Easter and ends on Pentecost. These practices help us remember the events of Jesus' Passion, Death, and Resurrection and the importance of celebrating new life in Christ. Here are just a few of the Easter traditions Christians practice. Easter eggs. In the past, eggs were not allowed during Lent, so people would save them and color them red to symbolize Easter joy. The Easter Rabbit is a very old idea that did not come from Christians. But now Christians use the idea of the Easter Rabbit to celebrate Christ as well. The tradition is that the rabbit lays the eggs in the garden and that is why children have to go and find them. Easter fire. The Easter fire is lit on Holy Saturday to symbolize the resurrection of Christ, and the pillar of fire that led the Jews out of Egypt in the Old Testament. The fire is lit by the priest and then it is used to light the Pascal candle. Easter Vigil. The Easter Vigil is a mass that celebrates the resurrection, and it starts late at night after the sun goes down. The priest lights the Easter fire before the mass begins, and then everyone uses that fire to light their candles. The church is dark and only lit by candles for a while, and then the church is lit to celebrate Christ's resurrection. Later the adults who want to become Catholic get baptized and confirmed. Easter traditions, customs, and liturgical practices teach us about the connection between the Old and New Testaments and the unity of Christian communities worldwide.
