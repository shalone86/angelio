So, what are the Stations of the Cross? This is the Catholic Q&A and today's question is, what are the Stations of the Cross? The Stations of the Cross, also called the Via Dolorosa, or Way of Sorrows, is believed to be the actual path that Jesus walked when he was crucified. Faithful Christians would make a pilgrimage to the Via Dolorosa to walk the same path that Jesus walked. The faithful travel from station to station, stopping at each one, to say selected prayers and reflections. However, it wasn't exactly easy for every faithful Christian to make a pilgrimage to Jerusalem due to finances or travel issues or Muslims who had invaded and occupied Jerusalem for hundreds of years. So faithful Christians began erecting their own Stations of the Cross closer to their homes. The Way of the Cross is the summit of our salvation and it is a great way to remember the sacrifice Christ made for us and to remember how dangerous and harmful sin can be. Christ had to suffer this terrible torment because of our sins. It's easy to feel sorrow for our sins when we constantly remember what Christ suffered for our sakes. There have traditionally been 14 stations, some based on scripture and some based on apostolic tradition. The 14 stations are as follows. 1. Jesus is condemned to death. 2. Jesus carries his cross. 3. Jesus falls the first time. 4. Jesus meets his mother. 5. Simon of Cyrene helps Jesus carry the cross. 6. Veronica wipes the face of Jesus. 7. Jesus falls the second time. 8. Jesus meets the women of Jerusalem. 9. Jesus falls the third time. 10. Jesus is stripped of his garments. 11. Jesus is nailed to the cross. 12. Jesus dies on the cross. 13. Jesus is taken down from the cross and 14. Jesus is laid in the tomb. Although not traditionally part of the stations, the resurrection of Jesus is sometimes included as a 15th station. So what are the stations of the cross? They were originally a spiritual exercise faithful Christians engaged in during a pilgrimage to Jerusalem. No one knows when the original stations were erected in Jerusalem, but similar stations were erected around the Christian world as early as the 5th century. Faithful Christians make an actual or spiritual pilgrimage when they walk the stations of the cross to commemorate the ultimate sacrifice Christ made for our sins. It is a great way to do penance during Lent because it reminds us of the seriousness of the punishment and suffering that sin causes.
