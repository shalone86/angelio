# An Introduction to Prayer
# The old video's words (speech-to-text, Nov 2026; small mistakes possible). Watch & Sing id: an-introduction-to-prayer, made 2024-03-26.

What is prayer? Prayer transcends cultures and time. It is a universal act of asking, earnestly.
It's an application of the mind to divine things. It is how we seek union with God. The Scriptures
present prayer in various ways, interceding, mediating, beseeching. Prayer is also vocal or silent,
set out loud or simply a thought in our heart and mind. Through prayer, we recognize God's power and
our dependence, drawing on faith and hope. What do we seek in prayer? Spiritual gifts, temporal
needs, and protection from evils. Who do we pray to? While prayers often address God the Father,
Jesus Christ and the Holy Spirit, or all three, can be invoked as well. We can only pray to God, and
only God can answer our prayers. However, we can ask for intercession or help from friends, family,
saints, and even souls in purgatory. They can pray to God with us and help us get an answer from
Him. Despite knowing the future, Christ and saints still pray and use their foresight to influence
events through prayer. They do their best to bring about what is best, even if those they pray for
may not accept the blessings. Who can pray? Holy people, regular people, and even sinners can pray.
The only souls who cannot pray and receive an answer from God are those in hell. For them, prayer is
impossible. Intercessory prayer is when we pray for other people. We should pray for one another. We
should pray for both the living and the deceased. We should pray for friends and family. But we
should especially pray for our enemies. That is what Jesus did on the cross. He prayed for those who
were killing Him. Praying for our enemies is what Jesus taught us to do. In short, prayer bridges
divides and it unites us in a deep bond of shared hope and faith. We should pray every day and join
in with the prayers of the church at Mass or in Eucharistic adoration, or even by praying a rosary
together with the family. Prayer is essential and we should never forget it. Thanks for learning
with us. And until next time, may God bless you forever and ever.
