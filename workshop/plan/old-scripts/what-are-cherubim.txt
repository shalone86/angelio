Hey there, today we're going to talk about cherubim. These are special beings that are mentioned a
lot in the Bible. They're like angels, but they're a little different. So what do cherubim do? Well,
they have a special job. They guard important places or things. In the Bible, they protected the
Garden of Eden. Welcome to SD Case and Courses. Today we are learning about cherubim. Let's begin!
Suppose you ever hear someone mention a cherub. In that case, they're talking about a type of
angelic spirit that closely served God. The word comes from an old Assyrian word that means near
ones or personal servants. It's pronounced karab with a Q sound. Still, most people say cherub,
which is technically wrong but easier to understand. Now let's talk about cherubim in art. They are
often mentioned in the Bible as figures used in the furniture and ornamentation of the Jewish
sanctuary. They were sculptured, engraved, and embroidered figures used in various places like on
the mercy seat of the Ark and in the Holy of Holies. They were believed to represent guardian
spirits or keepers. In fact, cherubim were even engraved in wood and metal. They covered the
paneling of the temple, both interior and exterior. The walls of the temple had pictures of cherubim
and palm trees on them. These cherubim had two faces, one of a person and one of a lion. They looked
towards the palm trees on either side of them. But we don't know for sure what they really looked
like. Some people think they might have had faces like an ox, but we can't be sure. Let's discuss
cherubims and their appearance in biblical visions. Cherubims surround God in heaven. They are like
His helpers who carry His throne and show His great power. In Psalm 17, there is a story about God
coming to help someone in need, and He rides on a cherub and flies on the wind. In another book
called First Chronicles, the cherubim are like God's special chariot that He uses to travel through
the sky. In the book of Ezekiel, there are two chapters that talk about cherubim. These cherubim
have four faces each, a human face, an eagle face, a lion face, and an ox face. They also have wings
and hooves like ox. In Ezekiel's vision, these cherubim stood together with four great wheels
between them. Above them was a crystal expanse with a sapphire throne and a man's likeness sat on
it. This extraordinary part of the Bible has been interpreted in many different ways. The two
chapters that talk about the cherubim are Ezekiel chapters 1 and 10 and 28. From the Bible, we can
understand that cherubim were perfect, wise, sinless, and close to God. They were also beautiful and
happy and kind. Some people think cherubim are only symbols while others, like Catholics, believe
they are real angels. God put them at the entrance of paradise to help carry out His commands. The
book of Ezekiel and the book of St. John describe the cherubim as similar beings. It is believed
that they help carry God's throne in heaven. The Jewish people also considered the cherubim real
angels. You can read about them in the book of Enoch and the books of Esdras. The Christian Church
has believed in cherubim for a long time. They were considered special angels and were only
sometimes mentioned in the lists of angels. Later, people realized that the lists needed to be fixed
and the cherubim and seraphim were added. They are now considered to be the highest and most
important angels. Pope Gregory divided the angels into three groups, and cherubim are in the highest
group, which includes thrones and seraphim. They're called the fullness of knowledge because they
know a lot about God. To sum it up, we've learned about cherubim and their importance in religion
and culture throughout history. I hope that understanding these angelic beings can help you
appreciate the Catholic faith even more. Thank you for learning with us. Until next time, may God
