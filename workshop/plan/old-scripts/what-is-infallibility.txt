# What Is Infallibility?
# The old video's words (speech-to-text, Nov 2026; small mistakes possible). Watch & Sing id: what-is-infallibility, made 2026-11-04.

We all make mistakes, but can our church's leader the Pope ever be wrong? Learn more in this lesson.
I'm your host Shalom Kasin and let's begin in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy
Spirit. The catechism says that Jesus gave his church a share in his own infallibility. Infallible
means can't be wrong. Jesus is the truth. He shares this gift with his whole church. So that what he
taught the apostles is kept safe for you and for me and for all of us. Jesus told Saint Peter, you
must strengthen your brothers. Jesus prayed that Peter's faith would not fail. Then he gave Peter a
job. Help the other apostles stay strong. The Pope does the same job for the church today. The
Pope's special gift is for teaching a doctrine pertaining to faith or morals. Faith is what we
believe about God and morals are how God wants us to live. So this gift isn't for math homework, the
weather, or who wins the big game. A Catholic book says infallibility is not attributed to every
doctrinal act of the Pope. The Pope is a person like you or me. He can make mistakes and he goes to
the confession too. Yes, the Pope goes to confession. And only his rare solemn teaching for the
whole church is infallible. So can the Pope ever be wrong? Yes, if he's predicting whether we're
doing a math problem, but he can't be wrong if he teaches on faith or morals. And that is papal
infallibility in a nutshell. Thanks for learning with us. I've been your host Shalom, and until next
time, stay holy, my friend.
