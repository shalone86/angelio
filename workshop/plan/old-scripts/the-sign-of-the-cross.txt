Did you know that Byzantine Catholics make the sign of the cross from right to left? Which shoulder
do you touch first? They also use their hands to teach themselves about the Trinity. Want to know
more? Well, look no further with this lesson. I'm your host Shalom Kasin and let's begin as always
in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. The Byzantine catechism says what
the sign of the cross is. The Christian professes faith in the most holy Trinity with the sign of
the cross. When we make the sign of the cross we say who God is, Father, Son and Holy Spirit. It is
a prayer we say with our words and with our hands. The catechism tells us where the hand goes from
the forehead to the breast and from the right shoulder to the left. Byzantine Catholics touch their
forehead, then their chest, then the right shoulder, then the left. We trace the cross of Jesus who
died and rose for our sins. The catechism also explains the fingers. Joining the first three fingers
of our hand is a sign of our profession of faith in the Holy Trinity. Three fingers together are for
the Trinity. The last two fingers bend down to the palm and represent Jesus. He is God and also
human. Three fingers for the Trinity, two fingers for the natures of Jesus. When Roman Catholics do
the sign of the cross, they leave the hand open and go from left to right. But the catechism says
that every lawful way of prayer is of equal right and dignity. Roman Catholics touch the left
shoulder first, but neither way is wrong. It is the same cross and the same God and the church loves
both ways of prayer. And that was the sign of the cross in the Byzantine right in a nutshell. It is
a prayer that uses your whole body. You touch your right shoulder first and it uses three fingers to
represent the Trinity. Thanks for learning with us. I've been your host, Shalom Kasin, and until
next time, stay holy, my friends.
