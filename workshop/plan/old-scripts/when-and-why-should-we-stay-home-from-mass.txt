# When and Why Should We Stay Home from Mass?
# The old video's words (speech-to-text, Nov 2026; small mistakes possible). Watch & Sing id: when-and-why-should-we-stay-home-from-mass, made 2023-06-12.

When and why should we stay home from Mass? This is the Catholic Q&A and today's question is, when
and why should we stay home from Mass? This is a great question considering the current pandemic and
the fact that most churches in the US and around the world are closed to the public, not even
mentioning schools and other recreational areas. Most people are stuck in their homes. So now is a
good time to consider how important it is to physically go to Mass. And yes, even though public
Masses aren't available right now, normally we are obliged to attend Mass every Sunday and Holy Days
of Obligation by the command of our local bishop. But it's also important to know when and why to
stay away from Mass. First, let's go to the Catechism. CCC chapter 2181 states, quote, the Sunday
Eucharist is the foundation and confirmation of all Christian practice. For this reason, the
faithful are obliged to participate in the Eucharist on days of obligation and less excused for a
serious reason or dispensed by their own pastor. Unquote. Pretty much everyone in the US has been
dispensed by their bishop right now, but during normal times those who deliberately fail to meet
their Sunday and Holy Day obligation commit a grave sin. So right there in the Catechism we have
three clear instances when we should skip Mass. The first is a serious illness. That means anything
that's contagious and anything that physically keeps you from leaving the house, such as dizziness
or diarrhea. Unfortunately some people choose to go to Mass even when they're sick. Of course, some
people love Mass so much they'd hate to miss a single Sunday and that's okay as long as they
consider the other parishioners. If your sickness is contagious, stick to the back of the church and
avoid touching anyone or anything unnecessarily. This is also the reason why many bishops have
closed the churches. They know people love seeing Jesus in the Eucharist so much that they would
even come if it was dangerous. I'm just glad I didn't have to make the difficult decision to close
or keep churches open. Now let's talk about two other instances where you should stay away from
Mass. Infants and young children. Sometimes they get sick. Just like adults, when they get sick,
parents have to care for them and naturally might miss Mass. Lastly, if a priest dispenses you from
Mass, for whatever reason, you're in the clear as well. Now, some people might be thinking, aren't
there other situations where I might need to miss Mass? And of course, if you are in the military
and are on deployment, if you are an emergency responder or if there's inclement weather, these are
all reasons why someone might miss Mass. But even though it's okay to miss Mass sometimes, we should
aim our focus on going to Mass every chance we get. I know you've been seeing a lot of live stream
Masses lately and while we're stuck inside practicing social distancing, it is a decent alternative.
However, it's nothing like being there at the Mass in person. Do you know why we should go to Mass
physically? As often as possible. We should go to Mass often because the Eucharist is the bread of
life. Every time we take communion, we are connecting with Jesus in a most personal way and in turn,
He shares His grace and blessings with us. Mass is also where heaven meets earth. You heard that
right. The Holy Trinity, the Blessed Mother and all the angels and saints are there at every Mass.
It is truly the greatest and most common miracle there is. And you can't get all those blessings
through a TV, phone or tablet screen. It's the difference between video calling your mom or dad who
is in a different country or physically going there to hug them and laugh with them and to spend the
holiday with them. Think about it. Would you rather video chat Jesus or would you rather be right
there looking at Him, adoring Him, gazing upon Him? It's a world of difference. In short, there will
be times when you cannot attend Mass, but we should also attend Mass as much as we can daily if
possible. Because more time with Jesus could never hurt. This has been the Catholic Q&A and until
next time, stay holy, my friends.
