# What does Byzantine Mean?
# The old video's words (speech-to-text, Nov 2026; small mistakes possible). Watch & Sing id: what-does-byzantine-mean, made 2026-10-21.

Byzantium was the capital of the Eastern Roman Empire. It has also been known as Constantinople and
Istanbul. The church in Byzantium had a massive influence on Eastern Christianity. Therefore,
Byzantine was the name given to the Christian traditions that flourished in the Eastern regions of
Rome. Welcome to SD Case & Courses. Today's question is, what does Byzantine mean? Let's begin with
a prayer. Glory be to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever and to the ages
of ages. Amen. Now, on to the lesson. In the year 325, the Roman Emperor Constantine created a
second capital for his Roman Empire. It was a small city named Byzantium. Constantine renamed that
city Constantinople and it became the heart of the Roman Empire where the Eastern Church grew. So
for a little while, there were two capitals in the Roman Empire, the West and the East. And they had
separate rulers. In the West, German soldiers took over the Empire and broke it into many different
countries such as France and Italy. In the East, the Emperors remained in power for the next 1000
years. The Eastern Empire was known as the Byzantine Empire and it was a Christian country. The
church in the capital, named the Hagia Sophia, which means Holy Wisdom, and its bishop, known as the
Patriarch, became very powerful in the East. On the other side of the Mediterranean Sea, the primary
church in Rome, St. John Lateran, and its bishop, known as the Pope, became very powerful in the
West. Over time, the Western and Eastern churches started to have arguments and eventually they
split into two churches with different leaders. The Pope in the West and the Patriarch of
Constantinople in the East. Catholics believe that Jesus put St. Peter in charge of the church and
St. Peter put the Popes in charge after he died, so this church split was a big deal. Despite the
split, the Byzantine Empire continued to spread Christianity around the Eastern world. At the time,
Eastern Christianity was called Orthodoxy and the first major Orthodox missions included Bulgaria,
the Kivian Empire, and Russia. All these kingdoms became Orthodox. Many years later, some Orthodox
Christians began to rejoin the Western church. They began to believe that St. Peter and the Popes
were the true leaders of the entire church. This is where we get the term Byzantine Catholic. They
are Orthodox Christians who rejoined the Pope. They traced their spiritual heritage back to the
Hagia Sophia Church in Constantinople. So what does Byzantine mean? Byzantium was the capital of the
Eastern Roman Empire and it was renamed Constantinople. The Byzantine Empire lasted over 1000 years
and spread Orthodox Christianity across the Eastern parts of the world. Byzantine Catholics were
Orthodox Christians who rejoined the Catholic Church and the Pope in Rome. They maintained unique
spiritual traditions and practices that go back to the Byzantine Church in Constantinople. If you
have further questions, please contact your local Byzantine Catholic parish or go to bizcalf.org.
