Beginning the Church Liturgical Year, Advent from Advenire in Latin, which means to come to, it's the season encompassing the four Sundays and weekdays leading up to the celebration of Christmas. During the Advent season, we prepare our hearts and minds for Christ's second coming and celebrate His birth on Christmas. Christians have been interested in Jesus' promise to return since the early days of the Church, but Advent reminds us not to get caught up in predictions. Instead, we should stay alert and focused, not weighed down by the worries of the world. Advent is similar to Lent in that it is a time of preparation and penance, helping us to quiet and discipline our hearts for the joy of Christmas. As we get ready for Christmas, there are some changes to the Mass that we should know about. During Advent, the priest usually wears violet or purple vestments, except for the third Sunday of Advent when they can wear rose vestments. This is called Gaudette Sunday, more about that in another video. There might also be some changes to how the Church looks, like an altar that has fewer decorations, and an Advent wreath. As Christmas approaches, we spend the last few days of Advent preparing for the celebration of the Nativity of our Lord. During this time, the Church sings the Old Antiphons, which have been used for at least the last 800 years. These beautiful verses use imagery from the Old Testament to announce the arrival of Christ as the fulfillment of ancient hopes and our present ones as well. It's a powerful reminder of the significance of Christmas. So what is Advent? Advent is a special time that we spend preparing for the celebration of the birth of Christ on Christmas.
