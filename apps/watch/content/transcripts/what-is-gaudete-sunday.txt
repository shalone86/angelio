On Gaudet Sunday, the Catholic Church celebrates with music and flowers, which are not used during the rest of the Advent season. The priests wear rose-colored vestments instead of purple. This day is much like Leitari's Sunday, which happens in the middle of the season of Lent and shows that the Lord's coming is near. These traditions have been used for a long, long time in the Catholic Church. During Advent, the Bible readings in both the Divine Office and Mass frequently mention the Second Coming of Jesus Christ. On the third Sunday, which is Gaudet Sunday, additional signs of happiness are allowed to emphasize this theme. The Church introduces more joyful scriptures on this day. Instead of inviting the faithful simply to adore the Lord who is to come, the Church now calls upon them to worship and celebrate the Lord who is now nigh and close at hand. The name Gaudet comes from the scripture that is read at the beginning of the Mass, which says Gaudet in Domino Semper, which in English means rejoice in the Lord always. During Advent, we are encouraged to be joyful and prepare for the arrival of Jesus Christ. The Gospel tells us that Jesus is with us even if we don't recognize Him, even if we can't see Him. This season is a time of expectation and preparation for the celebration of Christmas and the Second Coming of our blessed Lord Jesus Christ. On Gaudet Sunday, we suspend our usual exercises of being sad and sorry for our sins to symbolize the joy that we should always have in our hearts as faithful followers of Jesus Christ.
