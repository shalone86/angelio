What's with all the bowing and kneeling? This is a Catholic Q&A and today's question is, why and when should we bow and genuflect? Blet. That's a fancy word for kneel. Right, according to the Roman Missal, a genuflection made by bending the right knee to the ground signifies adoration and therefore it is reserved for the most blessed sacrament. You mean Jesus Christ in the form of bread and wine? Exactly, except in a very few special cases everyone who passes before the most blessed sacrament is required to genuflect. So we should kneel anytime we're in a Catholic Church and we pass in front of the tabernacle? Indubitably. Bowing, on the other hand, is a sign of respect and reverence. In a historic sense, bowing down on someone shows you have lower status than the other person. Like bowing to a queen? Of course. The practice of bowing goes back to Jewish temple traditions but the current tradition of referencing the name of Jesus takes its origin from St. Paul who wrote, God greatly exalted him and bestowed on him the name that is above every other name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bend and every tongue confesses that Jesus Christ is Lord. The custom was formalized at the Second Council of Leions in the 13th century which decreed the special honor due, that whenever that glorious name is recalled, especially during the sacred mysteries of the Mass, everyone should bow the knees of his heart, which he can do even by a bow of his head. The general instruction of the Roman Missal which currently governs celebrations of the Mass goes beyond that and says that a bow of the head is made when the three divine persons are named together and at the name of Jesus, of the Blessed Virgin Mary, and of the saint in whose honor Mass is being celebrated. We should bow our heads during Mass any time the Trinity is named together, at the name of Jesus, at the name of the Blessed Virgin Mary, and at the name of a saint on their feast day. We should genuflect any time we pass in front of a tabernacle that holds the Blessed sacrament. These practices lift us from the mundane and serve as a convenient reminder that there are lofty realities that transcend and beckon us and that God is due all the respect we can muster, be it physically, mentally, or spiritually.
